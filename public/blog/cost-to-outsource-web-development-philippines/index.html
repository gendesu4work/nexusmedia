How much does it cost to outsource web development to the Philippines?

2026 price guide for business websites, landing pages, online stores and web apps built by a Philippine team, compared with US, UK and Australian agencies.

By the Nexus Media team, Manila · Updated November 21, 2026

Short answer

Philippine web development agencies typically charge under 25 USD per hour, compared with roughly 75 to 150+ USD per hour at many US, UK and Australian agencies. At Nexus Media in Manila, most website projects start from about 1,000 USD, and typical business sites launch in 2 to 4 weeks. Every project gets a free fixed quote.

Price depends on scope, not pages

A five-page brochure site and a five-page site with online booking, a members area and payments are very different projects. What sets the price is the number of custom features, integrations and how much content needs to be written or designed.

The simple formula is still hours × hourly rate. A good agency breaks the hours down per feature so you can see where the money goes and cut what you don't need yet.

The stack is the same worldwide: React, Next.js, WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Supabase and the usual hosting providers. You own the code, the domain and the hosting account.

Ongoing costs to budget for

  • Domain: usually 10–20 USD a year.
  • Hosting: free to about 25 USD a month for most business sites; more for busy stores and apps.
  • Platform fees: Shopify, Webflow or a paid WordPress theme or plugins, if you use them.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups, security patches and small edits, often a small monthly retainer.
  • Payment processing: charged per sale by Stripe, PayPal or your local gateway.

What to check before you hire

  1. Speed and SEO: ask for a PageSpeed score target. We aim for 95+ on mobile.
  2. Ownership: make sure the domain, hosting and code are in your name.
  3. Editing: can your team update text and images without a developer?
  4. Fixed quote: a written scope with a fixed price and a launch date.
  5. Time zone: Manila is UTC+8, so feedback you send in the evening in the US or Europe is often done by your morning.
